03 · Timeline compass
2008

Our story

From new deliveries to international boat shows

2008:

Heysea is founded in Guangdong by a small team of naval engineers with a single 20-metre mould and a big idea: build yachts the way shipyards build ships.

2009:

The first hull leaves the shed. The Heysea 62 is delivered to a private owner in Hong Kong and becomes the yard's calling card in the region.

2010:

A new 18,000 m² facility opens, adding an in-house carpentry workshop so every interior is built under the same roof as the hull.

2011:

Heysea signs its first dealer agreement in Australia and debuts at the Sanctuary Cove International Boat Show.

2012:

The Asteria line is launched — semi-displacement hulls tuned for long passages with low fuel burn.

2013:

Heysea achieves CE certification across its range, opening the door to European owners and charter fleets.

2014:

The 27-metre Asteria 88 is delivered, the largest composite yacht built by the yard at the time.

2015:

Infusion moulding becomes standard on every hull, cutting weight by up to 12% while improving structural consistency.

2016:

Heysea debuts at the Fort Lauderdale International Boat Show and appoints its first North American representative.

2017:

A dedicated R&D centre opens, pairing CFD hull analysis with full-scale tank testing in partnership with a local university.

2018:

The 100th Heysea yacht is delivered. Owners from 14 countries join the celebration in Zhongshan.

2019:

Heysea unveils its first steel-and-aluminium superyacht project, a 43-metre tri-deck built to class.

2020:

Remote commissioning and virtual sea trials keep deliveries on schedule while borders are closed.

2021:

The Vista series launches with hybrid propulsion options and a redesigned, light-flooded main deck.

2022:

Heysea returns to Cannes Yachting Festival with three yachts in the water and a sold-out order book for the season.

2023:

The first New Energy catamaran prototype completes 1,200 nm of open-water testing on solar and battery power alone.

2024:

Heysea enters the explorer-yacht segment, signing its first European contract for a 35-metre Explorer — delivery set for 2028. The Seaview 60, Heysea's New Energy catamaran, crosses to Europe for its official debut at the International Multihull Show.

2025:

A second yard opens on the Pearl River estuary, doubling capacity for 30–50 metre custom builds.

Once the decision is made, the journey continues.
